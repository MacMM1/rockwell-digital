Is your website costing you customers? 5 warning signs

It doesn't work properly on a phone. It takes more than a couple of seconds to load. It doesn't say clearly, within a few seconds, what you do and how to contact you. It hasn't been touched in years. Or you genuinely don't know the last time anyone looked at it. Any one of these is quietly turning visitors away before they ever get to the "am I interested" part.

A website launch checklist for small businesses

Before you go live: check every page on a phone, not just a laptop. Make sure your contact details are correct and clickable. Set up basic analytics so you can see if anyone's actually visiting. Check your business shows up correctly on Google Maps and Google Business Profile. Test every form and link yourself, end to end. It's a short list, but it's the one that actually matters — most launch problems come from skipping one of these five.

Google Ads vs Meta Ads: which should you start with?

Google Ads catches people actively searching for what you do — strongest for trades, services, and anything people look up when they need it urgently. Meta ads (Facebook & Instagram) are better for building awareness and reaching people who weren't searching yet but would be interested — stronger for retail, hospitality, and visual products. If you're not sure, tell us what you sell and we'll tell you honestly which one fits.

What actually affects how much a small business website costs

The number of pages and how much custom content each one needs. Whether you need e-commerce or booking functionality, not just information pages. How much is being built from scratch versus adapted from an existing structure. Whether you want ongoing management afterwards or just a handover. We don't publish a price list because these genuinely change the number — but we'll give you a fixed quote before any work starts, once we know what you need.

Signs it's time to redesign your website

Your business has changed but the site hasn't. You're embarrassed to send someone the link. It doesn't work properly on mobile. You can't update it yourself, or it's held by someone you've lost touch with. Competitors' sites look meaningfully more current than yours. One or two of these is normal — three or more usually means it's time.

Do you need a marketing agency, or just these two things?

Most small businesses don't need SEO retainers, social media management, and branding workshops all at once — they need a site that works and the ads that bring people to it. Get those two things right first. If something else turns out to genuinely matter later, we'll tell you honestly, rather than bundling it in because it's on the price list.
